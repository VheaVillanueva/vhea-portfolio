02 — Background

Engineer first, automator second.

Before automation, I was a Senior Software Engineer for 14 years — Java, MySQL, Oracle, and AWS, across Amdocs Philippines, Smart, and SLI. I've shipped systems that had to work correctly the first time and keep working without someone watching them.

That's the standard I hold automation work to. A lot of "automation" online is a fragile Zapier chain that breaks the moment a field changes. I build like an engineer: error handling, logging, and documentation included — not bolted on after a client asks why something silently failed.

I'm AWS Cloud and AI Practitioner certified, and I work across Make, Zapier, n8n, Notion, HubSpot, Airtable, and Google Workspace — usually with an AI agent layer on top that does more than autocomplete: reading context, calling tools, and returning structured data the rest of the workflow can act on.

04 — FAQ

Questions clients usually ask first.

How is this different from hiring a general virtual assistant?
Most VAs execute tasks manually inside the tools you already have. I spent 14 years as a software engineer before moving into automation — so instead of doing the repetitive task by hand every time, I build the system that does it, with error handling and logging built in so it keeps working when you're not watching it.
What tools do you actually build with?
Mainly n8n, Make, and Zapier for the automation layer, wired to AI (OpenAI, Claude) for anything that needs judgment rather than just data-moving. On the business-systems side: HubSpot, Notion, Airtable, and Google Workspace. Full stack is listed above.
How long does a typical project take?
Depends entirely on complexity — a single-workflow automation is faster than a multi-system build with AI decision layers. Rather than guess here, I scope an exact timeline on the discovery call once I know what you're actually trying to automate.
Do I need to know exactly what to automate before I reach out?
No. Figuring that out is part of the discovery call — a lot of the value is in identifying which manual process is actually worth automating first, not just building whatever's requested.
What happens after the automation is built?
You get the working system plus documentation — not just a demo that only I understand. Error handling and logging are built in from the start, so issues surface clearly instead of failing silently.
Is this a one-off build, or do you offer ongoing support?
Both, depending on what fits. Some clients want a clean one-off build with full handoff; others prefer ongoing support as their systems grow or new tools get added. We figure out which makes sense on the discovery call.
